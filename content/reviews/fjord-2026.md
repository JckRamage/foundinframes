LFF Film #21

It's no La Bola Negra, but this is a worthy Palme winner. Challenging, thorny cinema which lets the writing speak for itself. Probably why it is aggravating so many people; they think that it's taking a side, but nothing is further from the reality of the film.

If you are engaging with it as it is presented, it is so interested in challenging preconceived notions. Should you be sympathising with these people when you don't necessarily agree with their values, but they're being persecuted by a government unfairly? The film has so much meat on the bones. It is so my speed of film, a contemplative European drama. I just love this type of filmmaking.

I love how much it asks of you as a viewer to be an active participant in the story. This movie, at the end of the day, is an exercise in empathy on how far you're willing to extend your empathy to people. The protagonists are not particularly likeable, I guess depending on your point of view, but it was so interesting to completely immerse myself in their world and sympathise with them with what they were going through despite having very different values. To have a film so clearly weaponise progressivism and highlight how even these values which are supposed to be welcoming and open can be malformed in the hands of the wrong people. Likely another reason some are struggling with this, yet the film really isn't interested in the left vs right debate.

It's wanting you to engage with the structures, society, the way that we build our communities, and the othering of people who are trying to enter a community where we build up walls instead of welcoming people in. It’s tackling hypocrisy, and the way that we politicise people's lives. The film is so layered and nuanced and gets progressively harder as the film goes on, but I appreciated that, and it ends on a beautiful final scene that has sparked a bit of debate between me and some people.

The film is so chilly and cold and procedural, and the setting in Norway suits it so wonderfully. Some of the characterisation is a little hard to contend with as by nature of how they're written, you remain at a distance from them for a large portion of the film. But I would far rather have to do that work to try and understand people who's values and upbringing are so far removed from my own, to submerge myself in my own thoughts than be treated with a lack of intelligence and have surface level exploration. Deeply uncomfortable experience that rewards you for choosing to engage with its filmmaking what more could you ask for
