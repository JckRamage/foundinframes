LFF Film #7

If you have Penelope Cruz and Javier Bardem in the same movie, you can bet that I am seated. Despite the lukewarm reception to the film, and truly hating Zeller's previous outing, I thought this had potential. While I admit this isn't the greatest movie in the world and agree with a lot of critic reviews which are frustrated that this movie ends up in a place which is entirely contradictory, I do think that is dismissing a lot of the good that this movie has to offer.

Zeller directs the hell out of this. It is some of the most confident and self-assured direction of the year (even if this results in a convoluted mess) which from the first frame completely immerses you and locks you into this world. The strength of the film really lies in its performances. As if this needs to be said but Cruz and Bardem and some of the best in the business.

It's very easy to dismiss the strength of the performances because we've just come to expect this from them, but seriously, when you're watching them act opposite each other, there's nothing better. The film opens on like this five-minute sequence where it just holds on both of their faces, and I was locked in from there.

The movie then goes on to explore a lot of different ideas. I is a meaty movie. There’s a lot to do with class divide and gender divide, and how that arises in a couple, and the decisions that couples make for their careers. Specifically, it really tries to dig into this idea of the privilege of job security and why people work. Is it just for more money? What if they already have enough money? Why do they do what they do? Perhaps there is no reason, just a bigger project, a bigger piece of work, a step up the ladder is all that they want, but why?

In that regard, it's dealing with some really interesting things but it's dealing with too much that it doesn't have time to unpack everything, and by the time it reaches its ending, it is quite contradictory to the ideas which have been unpacked in the course of the movie, and that is where my disappointment lies. It doesn't stick its landing, despite the pieces it sets up being so intriguing.

Despite my issues it is a perfect example of how to set mood and tone in a film. The performances genuinely are incredible. In a year of Penelope Cruz performances, this might be her best. It’s just a shame it doesn't quite stick the landing.
