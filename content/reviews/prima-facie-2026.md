LFF Film #15

Given the acclaim of the play from which this film was adapted, this also being written by the same writer and the strength of Erivo as a performer, this film feels like it should've been a recipe for a complete knockout... it sadly fails to meet the expectations set by the play.

Cynthia Erivo is incredible, I do not think she's the problem here, she completely disappears into this role, which is quite hard to do coming off the back of a role as big as Elphaba, but she does it with ease. The film exists as an expansion on the play, the play only really takes place in the courthouse, this movie takes it outside of the courthouse and explores the woman's life a little bit more.

The film's first half is largely this additional section, where we leave the courthouse and learn more about her. This part of the film is completely uninteresting, it kind of meanders around in her life, we watch this unconvincing relationship and we watch her get SA'd. All of which adds nothing to the film

But when the second half kicks in, it becomes a little bit more compelling, and it makes sense because that is what the play was. The themes it's tackling, the surfacing of these ideas surrounding the way our law handles SA cases - all extremely relevant and timely.

It's hard not to be invested in her story and her trying to defend herself, but the big fault of this movie is that it doesn't really contend with the fact that she was defending men and now has to defend herself. You could have swapped out her job for any other job, and it would have just been the same sort of story about a powerful woman who found herself in this position.

The interesting thing about this story is that this woman now has to reckon with the fact that she might have let men go despite them having actually committed the crime, and the movie is never interested in exploring that. And I think that's a real shame given that that is the most powerful part of this movie. We never really get to grapple with her internally at all, all of it is external and in the courthouse, which is compelling, but it all comes too little too late.

The movie then ends with this really weird call to action, it's like it's breaking the fourth wall and trying to inform the viewer that the laws that we have set in place are wrong and it just doesn't work in my opinion. That revelation should come from character, it should come from the story. People shouldn't have to be told that directly, and I think it sadly sours the movie a little. Good performance let down by a perplexing screenplay and complete lack of interiority
