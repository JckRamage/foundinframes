What happened to making movies script-first, James Gunn?

The DC universe has had a pretty rocky start, but despite that, I was looking forward to Clayface. This departure from the usual superhero flick into something more grotesque, more adult-leaning. And while it certainly is those things, it's a mess.

Much like their other film release this year, Supergirl, this shares the exact same problem as that, in that it's chopped to bits. On the list of personal grievances I have with films, really poor editing is very high on that list. It really pulls me out of a film, and sadly that happened with this. The first 45 minutes are rough, precisely because it has been chopped to bits.

We are barrelling through plot so fast that you get no semblance of who Matt Hagen really is. A point in the film that really highlights this is when the talk show host is talking to him and says something along the lines of “There are so many people inside of you, but who is the real Matt Hagen?" And I would have loved to have known.

This felt like really flimsy writing where they inject a line like that into make you feel like they've explored this idea of him having multiple people inside of him and that delving into method acting, completely losing himself in roles, he loses who he is, but the film never really shows that. I wish they leaned into his love for cinema, for film, and for acting in general, because it would make the back half of the movie far more satisfying when he's feeling more fulfilled by taking on these personas.

Instead, what you get is this really reductive characterisation. He feels incredibly 1D, alongside most of the cast, to be honest. This is particularly true when it comes to Naomi Ackie, who I love as an actress, but she isn't given a lot to work with here. This very elementary story that surrounds the film just kind of relegates her to this female who is concerned about Matt towards the end of the film.

Despite me not loving the movie, I think there are elements that really work. The inspirations that this movie takes from body horror are really phenomenal. The horror in this movie is where it shines. I love how grotesque it is and really unflinching in what it wants to show you. It’s a deeply uncomfortable watch, but I like that. And the performances are great. I think Tom Rhys Harries really loses himself in this role, despite not loving what we get from Matt Hagen. To me though, I would compare this to something like Venom. It feels very akin to the Sony Spider-Man universe, and I don't mean that in the most complimentary way, a really basic story that fails to explore its central character in any interesting way. This is especially disappointing when you put into context that this is meant to be still the start of the DC universe, and I think it fails on that front as well. This movie takes place in Gotham, but you wouldn't know that. And I’m only saying this because we have comparison points of so many other movies that are able to make Gotham feel like a character itself, but sadly I don't think the characterisation of both the people and the world is any good here

I like where we get to with the last act, and especially the last few minutes of this paranoia surrounding Clayface. I just wish we leaned more into that horror and had a more compelling story
